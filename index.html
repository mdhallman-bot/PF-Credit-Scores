Connect the Dots

Credit behavior → credit history → credit report → credit score / creditworthiness → lending decisions and terms

Credit history is the record. A credit report organizes that information. A credit score is a number created from report information to predict risk. Creditworthiness is the lender's broader judgment of risk.

Utilization Lab — Same Balance, Different Meaning

Context: A $500 balance does not mean the same thing for every borrower. Scoring models can compare a revolving balance with the amount of revolving credit available. That percentage is called credit utilization.

Directions: Jordan has a $2,000 credit-card limit. Move the slider through several balances. Watch the utilization percentage change. Then explain what the percentage tells a lender that the balance alone does not.

Balance: $100 · Utilization: 5%
Key idea: Higher utilization means a person is using more of the revolving credit available to them. Lower utilization generally indicates less reliance on available revolving credit. There is no single 30% “cliff” where a score suddenly becomes good or bad.

Hard or Soft? — Why Was Your Credit Checked?

Context: A hard inquiry is generally tied to an application for new credit and may affect a score. A soft inquiry is not tied to a new-credit application and does not affect a score. Some real-life situations can be either, depending on how the company performs the check.

Directions: Read each life event and choose Hard, Soft, or It depends. Read the explanation after every choice. The events are randomized.

Unlock the idea: Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion are the three nationwide credit reporting companies. FICO stands for Fair Isaac Corporation and creates widely used scoring models. FICO is not a fourth credit bureau. A person can have multiple credit scores because different reports, models, and versions may be used.

Round 2 — Build the FICO Formula

Directions: Match each standard FICO category to its approximate share. Use each percentage once. Then reveal what each category means.

Payment history
Amounts owed / utilization
Length of credit history
New credit

Payment history — about 35% in the standard FICO framework. Paying credit accounts as agreed matters greatly.
Amounts owed — about 30%. This includes balances and revolving utilization.
Length — about 15%. Scoring considers how long accounts have been established.
New credit — about 10%. Recently opened accounts and applications can matter.
Credit mix — about 10%. The history of different account types can matter; you do not need one of every type.

These percentages describe the standard FICO framework. Their importance can vary depending on a person's credit profile.
